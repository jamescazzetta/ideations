# Design a testable offer

Read [the shared contract](../../docs/CONTRACT.md). Require the workspace idea's CONTEXT.md; read available MARKET.md, CHALLENGE.md, ASSUMPTIONS.md, EVIDENCE.md and existing OFFER.md. Own only OFFER.md, published through `append-report offer-design`. Preserve earlier versions and annotations; label revisions O1, O2, etc. No lifecycle state is required to draft a hypothesis, and the draft never advances state.

## Decide what is being sold

Ground the offer in the user's thesis. Identify the target user, economic buyer and buying authority, painful job, current workaround, and observable outcome. A service, productized service or software product is a delivery choice, not an automatic roadmap. If the idea has no commercial intent, say so and stop without inventing a business.

Keep one primary offer focused enough to test:

- Buyer and problem: frequency, current baseline, cost or consequence, with sources and unknowns.
- Outcome and measurement: what should improve, over what period, and how attribution could be checked. Revenue, time, cost, quality and risk reduction are all possible value hypotheses.
- Scope and format: the smallest useful pilot or deliverable, explicit exclusions, acceptance criteria, and an effort/cost cap. Do not propose implementation before the BUILDING gate.
- Price and economics: a proposed price or pricing test, who pays, estimated acquisition/delivery/support effort, direct costs and uncertainty. Separate revenue from profit; avoid invented ROI or guarantees.
- Credibility: actual relevant evidence, demonstrable capability and honest limits. A mockup, technical demo or competitor's success is not proof of this customer's result.
- Buying and onboarding friction: one plausible acquisition route, the next customer decision, necessary access/data, responsibilities, handover and ongoing support. Reduce unnecessary work without concealing obligations.
- Learning: the most consequential objection or assumption and one suggested test, linked to an existing assumption/experiment when available. Do not write their artifacts or run another skill.

The [offer template](../../templates/OFFER.md) is optional scaffolding. Mark statements as observed, sourced, estimated or untested. A proposed price is not a payment, and an attractive offer is not demonstrated demand.

## Stop after the offer

No research, outreach, proposal sending, account access, spending, payments, guarantees, implementation, state changes or automatic handoffs. Use existing authorization only for the work it actually covers. Recommend validation-designer when a test would change the decision; do not invoke it. Store actual later observations through promotion-gate or explicit human additions to EVIDENCE.md.
