# Learn from prediction errors

Read [the shared contract](../../docs/CONTRACT.md). Trigger on a requested retrospective after parking, rejection, abandonment, merge, shipment, unexpected success or unexpected difficulty; or on a request to review calibration across ideas. No automatic lifecycle hook runs this skill.

## Inputs and ownership

For an idea require CONTEXT.md and read available historical OFFER, ASSUMPTIONS, VALIDATION predictions, EVIDENCE, STATE/GATE and earlier RETROSPECTIVE.md. Own only that idea's RETROSPECTIVE.md and, when cross-idea synthesis is requested, workspace CALIBRATION.md. Append dated entries, preserving original predictions and user notes. Missing history produces “not recorded,” never reconstructed confidence or invented outcomes. A retrospective can document abandonment without changing state; flag any unresolved disposition.

## Per-idea record

Capture:
- Original thesis and strongest assumptions with links to dated originals.
- Expected difficulty, demand, and outcome as recorded BEFORE the result.
- Actual observed evidence, chronology and limitations.
- Prediction error: what was over/underestimated, or not assessable.
- Reason for stopping, succeeding or changing direction, distinguishing user explanation from assistant inference.
- For commercial work, expected versus observed acquisition effort, customer outcome, collected revenue, delivery/support time and costs, repeat use/purchases and reasons for leaving. Missing values remain unknown; distinguish gross revenue from profit and estimates from measured attribution.
- A practical lesson and a falsifiable adjustment to the next decision.

Include contradictory evidence and opportunity cost. Do not rewrite the original thesis to make the outcome seem predictable. If nothing has happened yet, state that no outcome comparison is possible; propose recording a prediction in the next validation plan rather than inventing a retrospective.

## Cross-idea calibration

When requested, compare existing retrospectives, giving sample count, comparable cases, counterexamples and uncertainty. Look for recurring overvaluation of technical differentiation, underestimated distribution/operations, real personal pain predicting validation, sustained domain interest, physical-product burden, or signals that predicted success. These are candidate patterns, not assumed truths. One or two cases support a question, not a statistically significant conclusion. Record selection bias: unrecorded abandoned thoughts are absent from the portfolio. Favor one actionable calibration rule over many generalizations.

## Non-goals

No status changes, diagnosis of the user, external research, new idea generation, automatic capture, or recursive review. Suggestions do not become enforced global rules without the user's decision.
